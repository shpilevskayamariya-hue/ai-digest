Not long ago, many mothers went through postpartum depression without ever having a name for it. They were told they were just tired. That it was part of being a mother. That they'd feel better soon. Some did. Many didn't — at least not without support they weren't offered.

That's slowly starting to change.

## The numbers look alarming — but the story is more hopeful

Postpartum depression diagnoses have nearly doubled in a decade: from around 9.4% of new mothers in 2010 to close to 19% by 2021. On the surface, that sounds like something is getting worse. But researchers and clinicians think it reflects something different — more awareness, more screening, more doctors actually asking the question.

For a long time, PPD was dramatically underdiagnosed. Mothers didn't know what they were experiencing had a name. Clinicians didn't always screen for it. And the cultural expectation was that new mothers should be happy, grateful, and coping — which made it even harder to say otherwise.

## What the statistics actually tell us

Mental health is now recognized as the leading cause of maternal death in many countries — not complications during birth, but the struggles that follow it. That's a profound shift in understanding. And yet, only about 26% of new mothers receive any education about postpartum depression before or after birth.

That gap — between how common it is and how little preparation mothers receive — is where a lot of suffering lives.

## You don't have to figure this out alone

Feeling low, disconnected, empty, or overwhelmed in the weeks and months after birth is not a character flaw. It is not proof that you're not cut out for this. It's a recognized health condition with real treatment options — and it responds well to support.

## Small things that can help

- If something feels off — not just tiredness, but a persistent heaviness or numbness — say it out loud to your doctor or midwife
- Ask specifically about postpartum mood screening if it isn't offered
- Know that the first step is just naming it; you don't have to have it all figured out before asking for help

If you're in the early months of motherhood and not feeling like yourself, please bring it up with someone. What you're feeling is real, and you deserve support for it. 💛
