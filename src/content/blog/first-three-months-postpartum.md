There's this image of new motherhood that floats around — a glowing mom, a sleeping baby, everything somehow fine. And then there's reality. The exhaustion, the blur, the feeling that something is just... off, even when nothing is technically wrong.

I want to talk about that feeling. Gently.

## What postpartum depression actually is

It's not a flaw, and it's not rare. It's what can happen when your body goes through a massive hormonal shift — right when you're also sleep-deprived and adjusting to a completely new version of yourself.

It doesn't always look like sadness. Sometimes it's numbness, irritability, or just going through the motions without feeling much at all. It can arrive quietly, weeks after birth, without announcing itself.

## How it can actually feel

You might love your baby but feel strangely distant from everything. Or cry at nothing. Or feel nothing when you expected to feel a lot.

Some days are okay. Other days, everything feels heavier than it should. We don't always have words for it — it just feels like something is off.

## Small things that can help

Let yourself be helped. Talk to someone about how you actually feel — not just how things look. Get outside, even briefly. Lower the bar for what counts as okay today.

And if you notice it's been more than tiredness — persistent disconnection, thoughts that scare you, or a heaviness that won't lift — that's worth talking to a professional about. Not because you're broken. Because you deserve support.

---

You don't have to carry this quietly. The first months are genuinely hard for almost everyone. If they've been harder for you — that's not weakness. That's a signal worth listening to. 💛
