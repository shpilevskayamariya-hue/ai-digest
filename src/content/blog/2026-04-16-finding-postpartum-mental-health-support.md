If you've ever tried to find a therapist or a mental health specialist during pregnancy or after birth — and ended up on a waiting list, out of pocket, or simply unable to find anyone at all — there's a reason for that. A significant, structural one.

Research cited in a recent US Senate budget priority report found that 96% of the birth-giving population in the United States lives in areas with too few perinatal mental health specialists. Almost everyone. Almost everywhere.

## What that number actually means

Perinatal mental health — the care that supports mothers through pregnancy, birth, and the postpartum period — is a specialized area of mental health. Not every therapist, counselor, or even OB knows how to support it well. And there simply aren't enough practitioners who do to meet the need.

That means that when you're struggling and you try to reach for help, you may hit a wall. Long waits. Out-of-network costs. Providers who don't specialize in what you're experiencing. This isn't a reflection of how hard you tried or how much you wanted help. It's a reflection of a system that hasn't kept pace with need.

## Why naming this matters

There's a particular kind of loneliness that comes from needing support and not being able to access it. And it's easy — especially when you're already exhausted — to interpret that as a personal failure. "Maybe I'm not trying hard enough. Maybe I should just be coping better."

You're not failing. You're navigating a shortage that affects nearly everyone in your situation.

## What can actually help while you wait

- **Your OB or midwife** — they may be able to provide initial support or refer you to someone faster than searching cold
- **Postpartum Support International (PSI)** — has a helpline and a directory of specialists who focus specifically on perinatal mental health
- **Telehealth** — can expand access beyond what's geographically available; some perinatal specialists work remotely
- **Support groups** — both in-person and online peer communities offer connection and validation while you look for individual care

---

Finding help shouldn't be this hard. But knowing why it is — and that it's not about you — might take at least a little of the weight off while you keep looking. 💛

*If you're struggling with your mental health during pregnancy or after birth, please speak with your doctor or midwife. They are often the fastest path to the right support.*
