## You Don't Have to Sleep When the Baby Sleeps (But You Do Need *a* Plan)

Everyone says "sleep when the baby sleeps," but that's hard when there's laundry, dishes, and a hundred things you've been meaning to do. The more useful advice: stop trying to catch up on *all* the sleep and protect *one* good window instead. Even a single 3–4 hour uninterrupted stretch does more for your recovery than scattered 45-minute naps throughout the day. Ask your partner, a family member, or a friend to take a feed so you can get that window. It's not a luxury — it's how you stay functional.

## Your Baby Is Already Telling You When They're Hungry

Before your baby starts crying — which is already a late hunger sign — they'll show you earlier cues: rooting around, sucking on their fist, getting restless. Following those cues rather than the clock means fewer tears, better feeds, and a lot less second-guessing. The WHO and the American Academy of Pediatrics both back feeding on demand — roughly 8–12 times a day in the newborn stage is completely normal and nothing to worry about.

If you're ever unsure whether your baby is feeding enough, it's always worth checking with your pediatrician.

*Source: [Parenting Science](https://parentingscience.com/newborn-feeding-schedule/)*

## Baby Blues vs. Postpartum Depression — It Matters More Than You Think

Most moms feel weepy and overwhelmed in the first couple of weeks — that's the baby blues, and it usually fades on its own. But if those feelings are still heavy after two weeks, or you're feeling disconnected from your baby, struggling to function day to day, or just not like yourself — that could be postpartum depression. About 1 in 7 moms experience it, and half never get a diagnosis. Reaching out for help isn't a sign something is wrong with you. It means you're paying attention.

If something feels off, please don't wait it out alone — talk to your doctor or someone you trust.

*Source: [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/9312-postpartum-depression)*

## The "Not Today" List Is a Small Thing That Actually Helps

A psychologist shared something this week I wish someone had told me earlier: instead of a to-do list that makes you feel behind, try a "Not Today" list — everything that can wait, written down and officially postponed. For moms with newborns, this is basically a survival tool. Not every day needs to be productive. Some days, keeping yourself and the baby fed and reasonably okay *is* the whole job. Recovery, rest, and one small moment that brings you joy count too.

*Source: [Good Housekeeping](https://www.goodhousekeeping.com/health/wellness/a70725971/burnout-prevention-tips/)*

## The Apps That Actually Help at 3am

When you're up wondering if your baby's sleep pattern is normal or whether that last feed counted — a good app helps. **Huckleberry** tracks sleep and feeding with gentle, personalized suggestions. **ChildSeed** combines growth tracking with age-appropriate activity ideas. **Jenova AI** answers parenting questions like a knowledgeable friend rather than a clinical manual. None of these replace your pediatrician, but they can calm the spiral when you need a grounded answer at an hour when no one else is awake.

*Source: [Jenova AI](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/free-ai-for-parenting-and-baby-advice)*
