There's a specific kind of anxiety that shows up around the six-month mark — a mixture of excitement and mild panic about starting solids. What do you offer first? What if they choke? What if they refuse everything? What if you do it wrong?

The good news: there isn't really one right way to do this. And most of what you're worrying about is very normal.

## Signs your baby might be ready

You don't start solids by the calendar — you watch your baby. The usual signs that they're developmentally ready:

- They can sit up with minimal support and hold their head steady
- They've lost the tongue-thrust reflex (where babies automatically push things out of their mouths)
- They're showing interest in your food — watching you eat, reaching toward your plate

Most babies hit this window somewhere between 5.5 and 7 months. If your baby isn't showing these signs yet at six months, that's okay — a little patience is better than rushing.

## How to actually start

Keep it simple. One food at a time, offered once a day, in very small amounts. A teaspoon of smooth sweet potato. A little mashed banana. Some soft cooked carrot.

There are two main approaches: purees (smooth, spoon-fed textures) and baby-led weaning (soft finger foods your baby picks up themselves). Many families end up doing a bit of both, which works perfectly well.

What matters less than the method: that mealtimes are calm, unhurried, and not a performance. Your baby is exploring — not eating meals yet. Most of what goes in their mouth in the early weeks just comes back out, and that's completely fine.

## What's normal (and what to expect)

- Faces. Lots of faces. Grimacing doesn't mean rejection — babies often accept a food on the eighth or tenth try that they appeared to hate on the first.
- Mess. Start when you have time and low expectations for cleanliness.
- Refusal. Some days they'll eat enthusiastically; other days nothing will interest them. Both are normal.
- Slowness. It might take weeks before solids feel like a real part of your day. That's okay.

## Small things that can help

- Eat together when you can — babies learn from watching you
- Offer breastmilk or formula first, then solids; this way hunger doesn't make the new experience stressful
- Trust your baby to know when they're full — don't pressure or coax
- Keep a relaxed mindset; stress at the table is contagious, even at six months

---

You're not going to get this perfect, and you don't need to. Just show up at the table with something soft, a bit of patience, and a lot of wipes. 💛

*If you have concerns about allergies, choking, or your baby's readiness, your pediatrician or health visitor can give you personalized guidance.*
