If you've been feeling like motherhood is harder than it looks from the outside — or harder than it was supposed to be — the data backs you up. Mothers across the board are reporting lower levels of mental wellbeing than they did a decade ago, and the reasons go well beyond personal circumstance.

## The numbers behind the feeling

A recent survey tracking maternal mental health found that the share of mothers reporting "excellent" mental health dropped from 38.4% in 2016 to 25.8% in 2023. At the same time, reports of fair or poor mental health increased by 64%.

That's not a small shift. That's a generation of mothers telling us, collectively, that something isn't working.

## What's driving it

Researchers point to several interconnected factors: rising economic pressure, inadequate parental leave, a lack of accessible childcare, shrinking social networks, and persistent racial inequality in healthcare access and support. These aren't individual failures. They're systemic ones.

That matters — because it means that if you're struggling, it is almost certainly not because you're doing something wrong. You may simply be carrying more than one person should be expected to carry alone.

## Exhaustion isn't weakness

There's a quiet but persistent idea in our culture that struggling as a mother means you're not grateful enough, not resilient enough, not trying hard enough. The statistics suggest otherwise. When three in four mothers aren't feeling mentally well, that's not a personal problem — it's a structural one.

Naming that doesn't fix it immediately. But it can change how you relate to what you're feeling.

## Small things that can help

- Tell someone how you actually are — not the version you think they want to hear
- If financial stress is a factor, look into what local or state support you may be eligible for; many mothers don't know what's available
- Try to identify one thing each week that genuinely refills you — not what "should" help, but what actually does
- If you have a partner or family support, be explicit about what you need; people often can't guess

---

The exhaustion you feel is real. The difficulty is real. And support — whether from people around you, from a therapist, or from your doctor — is something you're allowed to ask for. 💛

*If you're struggling with your mental health, please speak with your doctor or a mental health professional.*
