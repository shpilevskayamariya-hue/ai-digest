You've fed them, changed them, held them, walked them, swaddled them, sung to them. And they're still crying. Inconsolably, for hours. This is colic — and if you're in the middle of it, you already know that no description quite captures how relentless it feels.

The crying is real. Your exhaustion is real. And the guilt — the creeping sense that you must be doing something wrong — is real too. But here's what the research says: you're not.

## What colic actually is

Colic is typically defined as crying for more than three hours a day, more than three days a week, in an otherwise healthy baby. It affects up to one in four infants, usually peaking around six weeks and resolving — on its own — by three to four months.

A 2025 review published in *Nutrients* examining the causes and treatments of infantile colic confirmed what researchers have been building toward for years: colic is biological, not behavioural. It's linked to an immature gut, imbalances in early gut bacteria (microbiota), and the normal but still-developing nervous system of a newborn. It is not caused by how you're holding your baby, how often you respond to them, or anything you did or didn't do.

First-time parents often perceive colic as more alarming — which makes complete sense when you don't yet have the reference point that this will pass. But the condition itself is the same.

## What actually helps

The honest answer is: not as much as you'd hope. But some things do have evidence behind them.

The strongest evidence is for *Lactobacillus reuteri* probiotic drops, which studies show can reduce crying time in breastfed infants with colic. Fennel-based remedies have also shown some benefit. For formula-fed babies, lactase drops or a lactose-reduced formula may help if lactose sensitivity is a factor.

Common remedies like simethicone drops (infacol-type products) and acid reflux medication are widely used but haven't been shown to work better than placebo for colic specifically.

Comfort measures — skin-to-skin contact, rhythmic movement, white noise, a warm bath — won't cure colic, but they can help in the moment and help you feel less helpless.

- Ask your health visitor or GP about probiotics if you're breastfeeding
- If formula feeding, a trial of lactose-free formula is worth discussing with your doctor
- Skin-to-skin and gentle movement are genuinely soothing, even if not curative
- Tag someone else in when you can — listening to sustained crying is exhausting and affects your nervous system, not just your patience
